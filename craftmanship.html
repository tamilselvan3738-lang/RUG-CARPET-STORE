Artisan Heritage • 6-Step Loom Process

How Our Rugs Are Made: 6 Steps to an Heirloom

From mountain sheep fleece to the upright timber loom, discover the meticulous 9 to 18-month journey behind authentic hand-knotted carpets.

Stage 01
Step 01 • Raw Material

Sorting & Hand-Carding Highland Mountain Fleece

We exclusively source long-staple fleece from high-altitude mountain sheep. Hand carding aligns the natural crimp while preserving 100% of the sheep's natural lanolin oils—giving the rug natural resistance to red wine, coffee, and water spills.

Key Artisan Metric: Zero Chemical Bleaches or Petrochemicals
Step 02 • Yarn Creation

Hand-Spinning on Traditional Wooden Wheels

Machine yarn is sterile and uniform. Hand-spun yarn retains subtle organic variations in thickness and twist. When dyed, this natural variance creates the famous “abrash” effect—delicate, shimmering ripples of color that distinguish genuine heirloom rugs.

Key Artisan Metric: 100% Hand-Spun on Wooden Charkhas
Stage 02
Stage 03
Step 03 • Organic Color

Botanical Dyeing with Organic Plants & Minerals

We simmer our yarns in copper cauldrons with mountain spring water and wild-foraged botanical pigments: walnut husks for deep browns, madder root for warm crimsons, pomegranate skins for ochre gold, and fermented indigo for sapphire blue.

Key Artisan Metric: 30+ Historical Plant Formulas
Step 04 • Loom Architecture

Warping the Upright Timber Loom

The loom is the foundation of structural stability. Master warpers string over 1,200 vertical cotton warp threads under uniform tension. Proper warping ensures that your completed carpet lies flat on hardwood floors without ripples, bowing, or curling corners.

Key Artisan Metric: Tension Calibrated by Hand
Stage 04
Stage 05
Step 05 • The Knotting

Tying 400,000 Double Persian Knots by Hand

Every single pile tuft is an individual knot tied around two warp strands with a hooked weaver’s knife. A master weaver completes approximately 6,000 knots per day. An 8×10 ft carpet requires 6 to 12 months of daily patient devotion.

Key Artisan Metric: Over 400,000 Knots per Carpet
Step 06 • The Finishing

River Washing, Sun-Drying & Hand-Shearing

Once cut from the loom, the carpet is washed with soft mountain spring water and wooden paddles to remove loose pile. It is dried naturally under alpine sunlight to stabilize botanical pigments, then hand-sheared with precision curved blades for velvet-smooth depth.

Key Artisan Metric: 100+ Year Expected Lifespan
Stage 06

Certified Lifetime Authenticity

Every rug shipped from our atelier includes an archival certificate of provenance, stating the artisan guild, origin region, knot count, and fiber analysis.

The Science of Knot Density: Why Density Means Forever

In authentic hand-knotted textiles, density is calibrated in Knots Per Square Inch (KPSI). A higher knot density allows microscopic curvilinear motifs, tighter fiber compaction, and unprecedented durability spanning generations.

Archival Care • 100-Year Protocol

Caring for an Heirloom: 4 Preservation Rituals

Because authentic hand-knotted mountain wool contains rich natural lanolin, maintaining your carpet requires surprisingly little effort when handled with proper care.

01

Annual 180° Rotation

Rotate your rug 180 degrees once every 6 to 12 months. This balances natural sunlight exposure and distributes foot traffic evenly across both ends of the pile.

02

Pure Suction Vacuuming

Always use a straight-suction nozzle or canister vacuum wand. Avoid motorized spinning beater bars, which pull on delicate hand-sheared wool tips.

03

The Lanolin Spill Defense

If wine or coffee spills, do not panic or scrub. The sheep's natural lanolin prevents immediate absorption. Blot gently with a dry, clean white cotton towel.

04

5-Year Mountain Wash

Every 4 to 6 years, treat your rug to professional hand washing with cold spring water and olive oil soap. This revitalizes the wool fibers and restores their natural luster.
